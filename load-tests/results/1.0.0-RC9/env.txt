WARNING — the JMH numbers in this directory are not comparable to earlier releases
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
They were captured on a machine that was not quiet, and it shows. Two runs of the *identical*
RC9 build, back to back, minutes apart:

  benchmark                          run A       run B     spread
  buildServiceFileMap                 35.2        23.3      1.51x
  resolveActiveServices_allPresent  5687.3      9086.6      1.60x
  resolveActiveServices_nonePresent 3874.9      7479.7      1.93x
  writeFileIfChanged_largeWrite    10408.6      7762.0      1.34x
  writeFileIfChanged_noChange       1810.4      1541.2      1.17x
  writeFileIfChanged_smallWrite    11167.7      7176.1      1.56x

A noise floor of 1.17x-1.93x swallows most differences anyone would want to read off these
numbers. For scale, 0.9.7 re-measured on this same machine today came out 1.4x to 3.1x slower
than 0.9.7's own recorded baseline — so the recorded history is not a fixed yardstick either.

What survives that noise floor, measured 0.9.7 vs RC9 in one session:

  resolveActiveServices_nonePresent  +438%   > noise, likely real
  resolveActiveServices_allPresent   +187%   > noise, likely real
  writeFileIfChanged_smallWrite      +110%   borderline
  writeFileIfChanged_largeWrite       +46%   inside noise
  buildServiceFileMap                 +45%   inside noise
  writeFileIfChanged_noChange         +24%   inside noise

The two resolveActiveServices results have a plausible mechanism rather than only a number:
that method stats one path per registered service to decide which are opted in, and the service
count grew from the 0.9.x era to 50 active in RC9. More services means more stat calls, so some
increase is the expected cost of the platforms added since, not a defect. Whether the increase
is *only* that has not been established here and should not be claimed.

Before quoting any hot-path figure from this directory, re-capture it on a quiet machine.

The allocation numbers are a different matter
---------------------------------------------
MemoryVolumeStressTest measures allocated bytes via ThreadMXBean, not elapsed time, so it does
not care that the box was busy. Two RC9 runs agreed to within 0.6 % at N=100/500/1000. Those
numbers, and the same-session comparison built from them, are safe to quote. N=10 is not: two
runs of one build gave 2995 KB and 12187 KB.
