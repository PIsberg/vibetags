What was under test
-------------------
1.0.4 is NOT published to Maven Central. The newest published release is 1.0.3. The jar
measured here was built from main @ 341b655 and installed locally, so this baseline covers
1.0.4 *plus* everything merged since the "prepare release v1.0.4" commit: Kotlin/kapt support
(#394), vibetags-cli (#395/#396), Groovy support (#397) and the CLI symlink guard (#398).
Every one of those is an example consumer, a separate CLI module, or a guard in the CLI —
none touches the processor's write path, which is why no movement was expected here and
none was found.

Confirmed rather than assumed, via `mvn dependency:tree`:
    se.deversity.vibetags:vibetags-processor:jar:1.0.4:compile

Skipped, not passed
-------------------
The sweeps ran with -Dstress.max.classes=1000, matching every earlier baseline. Surefire
reports "Tests run: 13, Skipped: 4": those four are the N=5000 and N=10000 cases, skipped by
the cap. They were not run and nothing here says anything about them.

ProcessorTaxStressTest and SignatureCaptureStressTest were not run in this capture. The

measures javac, not VibeTags — but it was not re-measured, and this directory therefore has
no processor-tax.txt of its own.

What survives measurement — the allocation comparison
-----------------------------------------------------
Measured back-to-back in one session, switching only -Dprocessor.version (see
memory-same-session.txt). OverheadAlloc(KB), lower is better:

  N       1.0.3      1.0.4     delta
  100     23425      23420     -0.02 %
  500    110545     110277     -0.24 %
  1000   219067     219828     +0.35 %

All three are inside the 0.6 % run-to-run floor this harness reproduces at, so the honest
statement is "no measurable allocation change between 1.0.3 and main", not "identical".

N=10 is excluded on purpose. Two runs of one build have given 2995 KB and 12187 KB there,
and this capture's two sweeps of it disagree by 4.3x (3021 KB in the standalone sweep,
13046 KB in the same-session run). Cache initialisation amortises poorly on ten classes.

Output size — the correctness check inside the perf report
----------------------------------------------------------
OutputSize(B) is byte-identical to 1.0.0-RC9 at every N:

  N=10    14179        N=100   101296
  N=500   495656       N=1000  988897

That is the useful reading of this capture: the work product did not move. Note that these
are NOT the values results/README.md long quoted as constant across all releases
(17156 / 122555 / 599895 / 1196918). Those held through 0.9.7; the number changed at
1.0.0-RC1 (14214 / 101607 / 497199 / 991983) and again at 1.0.0-RC9, where it has stayed.

WARNING — the wall-clock and JMH numbers here are noise
--------------------------------------------------------
Worse than RC9's, which already carried this warning. Several JMH benchmarks came back with
an error bar larger than the score itself:

  benchmark                            1.0.0-RC9              1.0.4        ratio
  buildServiceFileMap                 35.2 +- 17.5       26.6 +-  41.6     0.76x
  resolveActiveServices_allPresent  5687.3 +-4500.8    7034.7 +-8557.0     1.24x
  resolveActiveServices_nonePresent 3874.9 +-4800.6    1886.4 +- 151.1     0.49x
  writeFileIfChanged_largeWrite    10408.6 +-9239.0    4531.2 +-4473.9     0.44x
  writeFileIfChanged_noChange       1810.4 +-1055.8     923.0 +- 827.6     0.51x
  writeFileIfChanged_smallWrite    11167.7 +-3839.2    5740.9 +-6367.3     0.51x

Every ratio in that table lies inside its own confidence interval. The apparent 2x
improvements are not improvements; they are two noisy measurements taken on two different
afternoons. Nothing about processor speed may be read off this file.

The one JMH result that survives the noise is the cache-hit ratio, because it is 20x-200x
rather than 2x: cacheHit stays at 26-116 us/op regardless of body size while noCache runs
534 us/op (1 KB) to 23283 us/op (1 MB). That is the constant-time property the WriteCache
exists for, and it reproduces even on a loud machine.
