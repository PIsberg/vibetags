What was under test
-------------------
Not a release. `1.3.7-SNAPSHOT` is main @ 802d1420, v1.3.6 plus the unreleased descriptor-table
refactors (#762, #765), built under that revision deliberately, so that installing it did not
overwrite the published 1.3.6 jar in the local repository. That jar is one of the columns in
`memory-same-session.txt`, and a baseline that destroys its own comparison point is not one.

Confirmed rather than assumed, via `mvn dependency:tree`:
    se.deversity.vibetags:vibetags-processor:jar:1.3.7-SNAPSHOT:compile
    se.deversity.vibetags:vibetags-annotations:jar:1.3.6:compile

The annotations resolve at the parent's `${revision}` independently of `-Dprocessor.version`.
They are 44 SOURCE-retention `@interface` declarations with no code in them.

The previous baseline in this directory is 1.0.4, captured 2026-08-11, twelve releases back. That
gap is why this capture added the same-session comparison rather than leaning on the release trend

Skipped, not passed
-------------------
The sweeps ran with `-Dstress.max.classes=1000`, matching every earlier baseline. Surefire
reports "Tests run: 22, Failures: 0, Errors: 0, Skipped: 4". Those four are the N=5000 and
N=10000 cases of the two volume sweeps: they were not run, and nothing here says anything about
them.

Every stress class in the module ran in this capture, which the 1.0.4 baseline could not say:
it skipped ProcessorTaxStressTest and SignatureCaptureStressTest, and the other three did not
exist.

The correctness check inside the perf report fired
--------------------------------------------------
`OutputSize(B)` is 14180 / 101297 / 495657 / 988898. Every one is exactly one byte above the
1.0.4 baseline's 14179 / 101296 / 495656 / 988897. A constant, N-independent move means fixed
content, not per-element content, so it was chased rather than waved through.

Bisected by sweeping each release in one session: the value is 14179 through 1.3.4 and 14180 from
1.3.5 on. Rendering one probe class with the 1.3.4 and the 1.3.5 jars and diffing the six files
shows the whole delta in `gemini_instructions.md`: a blank line removed after the header and two
added before section headings, net +1 byte. That is #721/#723 in the 1.3.5 entry, "GEMINI.md's
wording and spacing ... sets every section off by one blank line". An intended formatting change,
not a defect, and now recorded against the number rather than left as an unexplained diff for the
next capture to rediscover.

What survives measurement
-------------------------
Read `memory-same-session.txt`. Its headline: the processor's allocation stepped up about 6 % at
1.3.2, bisected to f9b0e83f (#595), where `ModuleSidecar.unreadableSidecarNames` parses every
sidecar on disk a second time on every build. 1.3.5 gave back about a quarter of it. Main is
inside the noise floor of 1.3.6, so the unreleased refactors cost nothing measurable.

The processor tax
-----------------
`processor-tax.txt` reports the split at N=1000: javac's annotation-processing subsystem is
176881 KB of the 235033 KB this harness calls "processor overhead", leaving VibeTags' own share
at 58151 KB, 24.7 %. That agrees with the 1.0.0-RC9 capture's ~25 %.

A second run of the same test earlier in the same session reported 30.9 % (78004 KB of 252646 KB),
so treat the share as roughly a quarter, not as 24.7 %. Release-to-release *differences* in the
sweeps still mean something because the tax cancels; the absolute overhead figure is mostly
javac's and must not be quoted as VibeTags'.

WARNING: the wall-clock and JMH numbers here are noise
-------------------------------------------------------
Unchanged from every baseline since 1.0.0-RC9, and this capture is no exception: the eleven-column
same-session sweep put `Overhead(ms)` anywhere between 235 and 539 ms at N=1000 across builds
whose allocation agreed to 0.3 %. Nothing about processor speed may be read off `stress.txt` or
`jmh.json`, and no JMH comparison against another machine or JDK means anything.

The two wall-clock readings in this capture that are large enough to survive that:

  * `platform-breadth.txt`: 9434 ms for `all+granular` against 553 ms for `all-files`. A 17x gap
    is not measurement noise; it is 10058 files written instead of 56.
  * `jmh-cache-hit.json`: the cache-hit ratio is 20x-200x rather than 2x, and reproduces on a loud
    machine. That is the constant-time property the WriteCache exists for.

Windows @TempDir cleanup
------------------------
`concurrent.xml` reports errors="0", as every baseline from 0.9.7 onwards does. The cleanup
failure documented as a standing Windows caveat was real through 0.8.0 and has not recurred.
